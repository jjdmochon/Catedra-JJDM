 	   **SUMMARY**   	

Operating at the intersection of academia and commercial enterprise, Juan J. His international scholarly career spans institutions across Spain, Italy, and the United Kingdom, yielding a portfolio of nearly one hundred peer-reviewed publications alongside fifteen co-invented patents. He received his doctorate in Pharmacy from the University of Granada, where, after holding various research positions at the universities of Southampton and Edinburgh, he currently serves as an Associate Professor within the institution.

**PhD in Pharmacy** 	2001 to Current   
JJ Díaz-Mochón is a prolific researcher with over 90 peer-reviewed publications and a H-index of 28 in the field of chemical biology and organic chemistry, with a focus on combinatorial chemistry, enzymatic assays, and molecular biology techniques. His early work, dating back to 2003, laid the foundation for parallel and multiplexed bead-based assays. In 2004, he explored the full orthogonality between Dde and Fmoc, contributing to the world-first synthesis of PNA encoded peptide libraries. His work on polymer microarrays in 2006 led to the identification of substrates for hepatocyte culture. He has made significant contributions to the development of microarray platforms, particularly for enzymatic and cell-based assays, which have been cited extensively. His invention of dynamic chemistry nucleic acid detection, which is the basis of DESTINA, has generated over 20 peer-reviewed publications and is one of the most recent fundamental discoveries in nucleic acid chemistry in the last decade. He is also very active in the field of liquid biopsy with key discoveries such as the finding of circulating lung cells in patients with EPOC, which can also be used as a prognostic in COVID patients, and circulating nucleic acids and exosomes. Finally, his research also includes medicinal chemistry, in particular the development of new synthetic methods to obtain polysubstituted purines with potential antiparasitic and anticancer activities, as demonstrated in studies of Chagas disease and cancer.
